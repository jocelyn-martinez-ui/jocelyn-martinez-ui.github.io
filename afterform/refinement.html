PHASE 01

Project audit

KEEP

  • Repair, rewear and record as one service concept
  • Plum, vermilion, lilac and poplin
  • Bodoni Moda, Karla and Inconsolata
  • Plain-language repair index with a first-class assessment option
  • Explicit scope comparison and revision-sensitive approval
  • Five varied A3 poster compositions
  • Original material photography and contextual application studies

REFINE

  • Replace text-only logo rules with actual construction and small-size specimens
  • Give label variants a fixed information grammar and production dimensions
  • Expand editorial pacing beyond the existing twelve-page introduction
  • Show actual artwork beside contextual imagery
  • Make small functional print more readable than the first edition's tiny disclaimers

EXPAND

  • Responsive identity assets and a transferable brand manual
  • A 24-page second edition of On keeping
  • An operational print kit that carries a request through assessment and return
  • Window, directory and desk information as a coherent environmental hierarchy
  • Role-specific social formats and opt-in motion
  • A paper brief generated from saved prototype data
  • A returning-garment flow linked to its earlier sample record

REPLACE

  • The first edition's thin typographic favicon at the smallest sizes
  • Unspecified stamp and sticker opportunities with only functional review labels and a record-envelope seal
  • Mockup-first case-study sequencing with inspectable identity, editorial and UI evidence
  • Seven-screen references with nine connected screen studies

The second pass preserves the concept and its strongest work. The opportunity is depth: clearer identity rules, more complete editorial content, real operational graphics and two useful additions to the product.

PHASE 02

Visual identity expansion

Positioning: a garment-repair studio that treats emotional value and practical clarity as equally important. The personality stays candid, material-aware, expressive and considerate.

Logo architecture: retain the outlined primary wordmark. Add a horizontal service lockup, stacked lockup, compact social avatar and small joining symbol. The micro mark is a functional reduction, not an invented certification seal.

Typography: Bodoni Moda establishes character; Karla carries tasks and directions; Inconsolata supports IDs and revisions. The primary mark is a type-based design using licensed outlines. The selected tracking is restrained, and the 16 px symbol removes delicate display strokes.

DecisionAlternative consideredTradeoff and validation
Keep the current paletteIntroduce another fashion paletteContinuity makes the project recognisable. Distinction comes from better behaviour and composition, not extra colours.
Simplify the micro markShrink the full wordmarkLess naming information; requires the full name in the surrounding interface. Test recognition at 16, 24 and 32 px.
Explicit colour pairsUse every colour on every backgroundLimits some expressive combinations to preserve readable information. Print colour still needs a stock-specific proof.
PHASE 03

Graphic language

Design grammar: name the piece; show one relevant change; keep the record. A stable garment ID, a repair description and a next-action zone organise the system. A joining line can be continuous, visibly interrupted or awaiting advice; it never replaces its text label.

Ten functional icons now share a 24 px grid and 1.75 px square-ended strokes. Six existing action icons are retained; print, label, revise and garment extend the family. No decorative arrows, fake barcodes, invented wash symbols or certification marks are introduced.

The label workbench shows four workflow states, three garment records, monochrome output and a long-content specimen. Long content moves to a taller tag instead of forcing smaller type. A 4 mm tag hole is separated from the wordmark and all essential information.

PHASE 05

Editorial & print

On keeping: expanded from twelve to twenty-four pages while retaining the original edition. The new object includes an inside cover, contents, opening spread, image-led study, two finish directions, taxonomy, intake explanation, request/proposal comparison, decision paths, physical/digital record, care boundaries, checklist, colophon and back cover.

Production concept: 170 × 240 mm, saddle stitch, six folded sheets, 16 mm outer margins and a 6 mm spacing rhythm. Proposed stock: 120 gsm uncoated text and 200 gsm cover. These are proposed specifications; grain, creep, gutter, colour and binding need a paper proof and printer review. The PDF is in reading order.

Operational print: twelve pieces include an A5 intake brief, A5 comparison, 100 × 150 mm work ticket, 72 × 115 mm tag front and reverse, four 90 × 65 mm workflow labels, a 50 mm seal, a 60 × 80 mm spare-fastening envelope net and an A6 follow-up card. The previous return band and sleeve remain available.

Material logic: keep labels on removable packaging; do not put untested adhesive on garments. The envelope carries a spare fastening and record ID. It has a 20 mm closing flap, 10 mm glue tab and 15 mm bottom flap; the drawn net is a prototype for fit testing.

PHASE 06

Campaign & communication

The five existing A3 posters already demonstrate strong variation: typographic invitation, garment image, finish comparison, process and repair index. They are kept.

PHASE 07

Information & environmental design

A four-level environmental hierarchy distinguishes invitation, navigation, conversation and handover. A 600 × 900 mm window graphic introduces the service; a 500 × 400 mm directory names three destinations; an A5 landscape counter sign normalises questions; a 400 × 200 mm collection sign connects the piece to its record.

Information design carries the experience: the repair index preserves plain-language labels, request and proposal appear side by side, and label zones separate object identity from repair type and changing status. A revision is never confused with a permanent garment ID.

The arrows are explicitly a direction study. A real site plan, mounting height, glare, lighting, accessibility review and distance test are necessary before installation. A geographical map and fabricated address would have no legitimate role.

PHASE 08

UX expansion

Users: a person who wants to keep a garment but lacks repair vocabulary, and a studio that needs a clear description without mistaking a preference for an achievable result. These are working assumptions, not interview findings.

Journey: a window or campaign introduces repair; the desk helps describe the piece; the printable brief carries that intention; assessment adds practical limits; the comparison supports a decision; the tag and record support handover and a later request.

Architecture: Find a repair → service detail → three-step request → My request → proposal or paper brief. The garment-record area supports typed lookup, individual records, downloads and a linked new assessment. Screen-study URLs are isolated from saved visitor data.

Boundary: no accounts, shipping, scheduling, payment or live studio messaging. Those would require operational evidence and a real service. The prototype continues to use local storage with an explicit in-memory fallback.

PHASE 09

UI expansion

Nine interactive studies now cover discovery, garment details, finish, review, proposal, garment record, empty search, paper brief and returning intake. The new print view uses a precise three-zone hierarchy and carries the request ID, revision, garment, repair type, place, material and preference.

On mobile, product controls remain ahead of imagery; the step rail becomes compact; comparisons stack in reading order. The paper brief collapses metadata into a single column. The label workbench moves controls above the specimen. Editorial spreads become readable single pages rather than tiny facing pages.

Focus-visible controls, persistent labels, native radio groups, validation messages, status announcements, confirmation dialogs and missing-route recovery remain. The record lookup now clears stale error semantics when the ID is edited.

PHASE 11

Motion

The motion specimen plays only when requested. A joining line progresses between the two brand statements over 1.2 seconds, then becomes still. Stop shows the final composition immediately. Reduced-motion preferences skip the movement entirely.

UI feedback uses 160 ms colour/border transitions and a 280 ms joining reveal where appropriate. Navigation does not depend on animated entrances, and feedback does not wait for a decorative sequence.

Loading is presented as an explicit demonstration, not a fabricated backend request. The prototype does not add an artificial delay to work that completes locally.

PHASE 12

Accessibility

Digital: the retained palette provides 13.32:1 for plum/poplin, 8.88:1 for plum/lilac and 5.15:1 for vermilion/poplin. Vermilion/lilac is 3.43:1 and is restricted to large display text; it does not carry small instructions. Focus, text labels and selected borders provide non-colour cues.

Reading: UI body remains 17 px; regular labels target at least 14 px. The record uses small secondary metadata only when it is not the primary instruction. The publication has a complete HTML text alternative. New icon buttons are not introduced without names.

Physical: the new larger tags protect essential 9 pt type. Uncoated, low-glare materials are proposed. Actual-size reading, hole clearance, folds, adhesive, QR scanning and environmental viewing distances still need a physical proof.

Verification boundary: source-level and DOM checks can verify flows, state changes, links and contrast calculations. They do not establish browser layout quality, assistive-technology usability or WCAG conformance. Manual browser, keyboard, screen-reader and print tests remain necessary.

PHASE 13

Case-study art direction

The case study now starts with the identity and the actual service premise, then exposes the complete touchpoint journey. It alternates a dense system board, a quiet explanation, a full editorial spread, small paper objects, a product sequence and a contextual physical view.

Flat work is primary evidence: recruiters can inspect outlined marks, label hierarchy, publication pages, environmental typography and working interface states. Contextual images support material and scale; they do not conceal the artwork or stand in for production files.

Captions identify what an image proves. Generated material and application images remain labelled as illustrative. The portfolio contains no fictional customers, interviews, metrics, launches or installed signage.

PHASE 14

Hero assets

Editorial moment: pages 4–5 pair a vermilion typographic statement with a large garment crop and one specific caption. Pages 6–7 move from material detail to the limits of assessment. They are shown as actual facing artwork, with no device frame.

Brand moment: the system board combines the established wordmark, compact signature, garment ID, next-action label and colour roles. The composition is authored as SVG, so its text and spacing remain exact.

Product moment: the nine-state explorer connects request, proposal and brief. The central comparison is live, with question, approval, undo and revision behaviour.

Physical moment: the existing return-kit application sits beside the actual band, sleeve, new spare-fastening net and record seal. The image is a contextual illustration; the flat designs remain authoritative.

Ecosystem moment: a final composition pairs the identity board with publication, intake brief, tag and window artwork. Each image also links to its full page or source PDF so a viewer can inspect the detail.

What still needs real evidence

Observe how people describe a damaged garment. Ask repairers what information they need before assessment. Test whether people distinguish a preference, a proposal and approval. Print and scan the tags at actual size. Fold the packaging nets. Test the responsive prototype with keyboard and assistive technology.

These gaps cannot be solved honestly by inventing participants or success metrics. The new work supplies specific material for validation; it does not claim validation happened.

Five memorable decisions

  1. A joining rule expresses the repair and becomes a functional finish distinction.
  2. A stable ID connects paper, product and a later conversation.
  3. A label grows before its essential text shrinks.
  4. A changed proposal requires a new decision.
  5. The brand can be expressive while the task remains quiet.
