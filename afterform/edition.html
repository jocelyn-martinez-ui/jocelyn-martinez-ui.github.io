A REPAIR BEGINS WITH A PERSON.

Something

you reach for.

Not every piece has a grand story. Sometimes it is simply the shirt that fits, the jacket you borrow or the jeans you already know.

This guide helps you describe a change without needing the technical name. The actual garment and a repairer determine what is possible.

07 / Material study / what the cloth decides

LOOK BEFORE YOU LABEL IT.

The cloth

has a say.

A photo can explain where the problem is. It cannot confirm the strength of the surrounding cloth, the feel of the repair or the best method.

Describe, do not diagnose.

The studio needs your observation, not an expert conclusion.

Keep uncertainty visible.

“Not sure” is a useful answer about fibre, method or finish.

08 / Finish / closest practical match

02 / THE CHOICE

Quiet repair.

Let attention stay on the piece.

A close colour match and familiar construction can reduce the contrast of a repair. It may still be visible, and the material may feel different.

ASK BEFORE YOU AGREE

What is the closest practical match? What will still be visible? Could the finish affect how the piece falls?

09 / Finish / deliberate contrast

TWO INTENTIONS. NO PROMISED INVISIBILITY.

New

character.

A contrasting thread, patch or fastening can make the repair part of the piece. The colour is a preference; suitability still depends on the garment.

Ask to see a sample. A beautiful contrast still needs a practical method.

10 / Taxonomy / useful codes

20 / Care / a boundary worth keeping

INFORMATION HAS LIMITS.

Keep the

original label.

The repair record adds context. It does not replace garment-specific care instructions, confirm an unknown fibre content or create new wash symbols.

Known

The garment ID and the documented repair description.

Still to check

The original label and any repair-specific advice from the repairer.

21 / Follow-up / something changed

Notice something

different?

Keep the record and describe the place that needs attention. A new request starts a fresh assessment; it does not rewrite the earlier repair.

Uncertainty is welcome at every stage.
