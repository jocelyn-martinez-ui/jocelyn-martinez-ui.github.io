Portfolio gap analysis

The starting collection contains seven distinct projects: INTERVAL, ImHere, Holland Walking Tour, Áurea, Loopline, Nutriva and Novella. The strongest existing pattern is a designer connecting information, interaction and visual character. The new project must add a different kind of object, audience and decision—not another festival, database or planning dashboard.

03 / 25

Selected concept

AFTERFORM is selected. It combines a believable local service with an object that moves between a customer and a studio. The object naturally needs a label, receipt, record, return band and guide. The product naturally needs uncertainty handling and explicit decisions. The graphic design and UX therefore begin from the same problem.

OFFCUT offers excellent specification complexity but would overlap the portfolio’s existing operational interfaces. POSTMARK offers valuable consent work but would repeat its literary/editorial territory. AFTERFORM opens a fashion-and-craft direction while remaining feasible as a local, buildless prototype.

The scope is intentionally limited to discovery, one local request, an example proposal and sample garment records. There is no invented repair business, real booking, payment, logistics service or repair outcome.

Keep the clothes. Change the ending.

The final direction uses no gradient. Each piece selects a dominant pair: plum/poplin for forms, plum/lilac for labels and the guide, poplin/vermilion for an editorial statement, or lilac/plum for a deep campaign surface. Red is not spread across every control.

07 / 25

Final creative direction

Second impression is an identity about keeping what works and making a considered intervention. The garment is still recognisable; the repair is allowed to have character. This idea governs both the visual language and the approval interaction.

The emotional layer uses expressive type and close cloth photography. The practical layer uses plain labels, linear fields and readable records. The two layers meet at the repair code and joining line. That connection keeps the work from becoming a branding exercise with an unrelated UI attached.

RuleExecution
HierarchyOne expressive statement, then useful information. Display type does not enter long labels or dense comparison text.
GridAsymmetric editorial compositions; stable alignment for the content that must be compared.
ColourPlum is the information colour. Lilac is the service surface. Vermilion marks a deliberate intervention.
ImageryOriginal illustrative cotton and denim repairs; soft side light, close crops and material texture. No invented before/after claim.
TextureComes from photographed cloth and physical-paper applications. No decorative grain overlay.
VoiceWarm, specific and candid: “Tell us what you notice,” “Closest practical match,” “Let’s decide together.”
RestraintNo sustainability totals, arbitrary coordinate stamps, clothing-shop checkout or unrelated merchandise.

UX strategy

Primary users: garment owners who want to keep a familiar item and may be unsure of the repair language. Secondary users: studio assessors who need to clarify scope and avoid an assumed agreement. These are audience definitions, not fabricated interview personas.

Core problem: the desired outcome, practical repair method and permission to proceed can become disconnected. AFTERFORM separates the request from the assessed proposal and makes the second decision visible.

Customer goal: describe what needs attention, understand the proposed result and retain useful details. Studio goal: receive enough context to begin assessment and record clear consent when the proposal changes.

Constraints: material and damage cannot be reliably assessed from a form alone; no live repair business or operational data exists; the front end has local browser storage only. Price ranges and proposals are explicitly illustrative. No account, payment or shipment is simulated as a real transaction.

ArchitectureContent and behaviour
Find a repairSearch plus All / Repair / Adjust / Assessment. Plain-language titles precede short codes.
Service detailPurpose, meaningful finish options, uncertainties and an illustrative range.
My requestDraft intake, saved request, editable snapshot and proposal review.
The garment recordTyped ID lookup plus three sample records. No fabricated customer account.
Supporting contentStudio guide, identity gallery and case study live outside the task navigation.

Assumptions to test: people can identify the garment location without a diagram; “closest practical match” sets a useful expectation; seeing both versions helps people understand the change; a detachable record is worth keeping. The implementation does not turn these assumptions into claimed findings.

Hero + thumbnail

The case-study hero pairs an oversized AFTERFORM wordmark with an intimate cotton-repair photograph. The name establishes fashion character; the cropped garment makes the subject concrete. A small caption carries the two-part campaign line. No laptop or floating-phone arrangement is needed.

The homepage thumbnail uses an asymmetrical material/typography composition with a small matching tag. The garment, lilac field and vermilion repair establish a recognisable identity before the title is read. The portfolio’s neutral caption and navigation remain the consistent frame.

The complete return-kit image appears later, when the viewer has enough context to understand each object. It is not used to hide the flat artwork. A publication spread, tag face, repair-code detail and actual packaging net provide deliberate close views throughout the presentation.

The memorable quality: an expressive identity that becomes useful information. The same code that anchors a poster helps match a garment; the same finish distinction that shapes the guide helps a person choose; the same candid tone protects the approval decision.

In an interview, the strongest discussion is not “I made many deliverables.” It is why each deliverable belongs, where the system needs a human assessment, and how the design prevents an attractive interface from implying a promise it cannot keep.

Priorities before presenting this as finished professional work

  • Essential: personally review and understand every decision; describe the project as self-directed and AI-assisted; run a manual keyboard/mobile pass before using it in applications.
  • High impact: test the language and proposal comparison with actual garment owners and repair professionals; print a tag and paper sleeve at full scale.
  • Polish: confirm a public portfolio destination, regenerate QR codes for that destination, proof colour on chosen stock and test signage at distance.

The work is already built as a coherent portfolio project. These next steps establish evidence and production confidence; they do not replace the completed design and implementation.
