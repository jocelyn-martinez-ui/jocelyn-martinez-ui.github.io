# Three concept candidates

This comparison preceded the INTERVAL product build. All candidates are self-directed concepts, not client briefs.

| Concept | User problem | Hiring value | Portfolio overlap | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| INTERVAL — a festival for close listening | New visitors must interpret an unfamiliar programme, assess the conditions of each session and build a schedule without overlapping commitments. | Temporal interaction design, access information, cultural identity, programme typography, print and spatial applications. | Complements the existing catalogue, civic, record and everyday-tool projects. | Selected: strongest bridge between substantive UX and expressive graphic design. |
| OFFCUT — a material reuse exchange | Makers need to judge whether surplus materials are suitable, reserve a manageable quantity and coordinate a collection. | Structured inventory, reservation rules, material-led identity, packaging labels and service communication. | Collection and handoff logistics repeat Loopline; ingredient-like inventory repeats Nutriva. | Keep as a future direction only if supported by real maker interviews and a material partner. |
| AFTERIMAGE — a community moving-image archive | Visitors need to understand provenance and viewing rights while choosing what to watch or attend. | Archival information architecture, rights metadata, editorial imagery and exhibition identity. | Catalogue discovery repeats Áurea and Novella; rights research is not currently available. | Do not add now; deepen archival metadata in the existing work first. |

## Why INTERVAL
The existing six projects show purposeful identities and useful interaction depth. The clearest new opportunity is a single identity that connects a digital task with a programme, physical pass and environmental wayfinding. The central UX decision is time: two interesting sessions may be incompatible, and removing an existing choice must remain deliberate and reversible.

## Research boundary
No interviews, audience counts, usability results, clients or launched festival are claimed. Reference review and implementation inspection support the concept; audience assumptions require future validation. Eight original sample sessions and three fictional rooms provide controlled interaction examples.
