01 / Overview
The opportunity

Make fragrance data feel as considered as the product itself.

Fragrance enthusiasts need practical information—notes, longevity, projection, season, and occasion—but dense databases can make discovery feel like research rather than pleasure.

Aurea explores a quieter alternative. The concept gives imagery and narrative enough room to create desire, while keeping performance details easy to find when the user is ready to compare. The goal was not to remove information, but to sequence it more thoughtfully.

01

Browse by feeling

Lead with mood, season, and visual character so discovery can begin before a user knows a specific brand or note.

02

Reveal useful depth

Keep technical details available without asking users to process every metric at the beginning of the journey.

03

Build a personal archive

Turn saving into collection-building, helping each profile feel expressive rather than purely transactional.

Project scope: Aurea is a self-initiated product concept. The interactive prototype demonstrates the proposed experience and visual direction; it remains a design hypothesis to validate through formal user research.
02 / Experience
The core journey

A four-part path from curiosity to collection.

The experience is structured around the questions fragrance shoppers naturally move through: What draws me in? How does it perform? What does it feel like? Do I want to remember or own it?

Step 01

Discover

Explore recommendations through mood, season, and visual storytelling rather than an overwhelming product grid.

Step 02

Evaluate

Review notes, longevity, projection, and wear context in a compact hierarchy designed for quick comparison.

Step 03

Understand

Read a concise editorial brief that translates abstract notes into an atmosphere, memory, or point of view.

Step 04

Collect

Save fragrances into a personal archive that supports identity, memory, and future purchase decisions.

03 / Decisions
Design rationale

Editorial atmosphere, anchored by practical hierarchy.

Luxury presentation only works when it still helps people make decisions. I used pacing, typography, and progressive disclosure to create a refined visual experience without hiding the information that matters.

Information hierarchy

Story first, specifications second.

Imagery, name, and mood establish an immediate impression. Notes and performance details sit one layer deeper, allowing the interface to support both casual browsing and informed comparison.

Visual pacing

Whitespace creates focus—not emptiness.

Generous margins isolate each bottle and key message. The slower rhythm gives distinctive packaging and typography room to carry the brand experience.

Data presentation

Translate performance into scannable signals.

Longevity and projection are presented as restrained visual scales rather than dense charts, keeping comparison quick while preserving the editorial tone.

Retention

Make saving feel like curating.

The collection view treats saved fragrances as a personal archive. This gives users a reason to return beyond completing a single purchase task.

04 / Visual system
A quiet luxury language

Soft materials, strong contrast, and intentional restraint.

The refined interface combines warm parchment, oxblood, dusty rose, and champagne accents to evoke perfume materials, archival paper, and intimate evening light. A characterful serif carries fragrance identity while a clean sans serif keeps comparison, filtering, and collection tasks precise.

07 / Validation plan
What I would test next

Move from a compelling concept to an evidence-led product.

Because Aurea began as a visual and interaction exploration, the next phase would test whether the hierarchy supports real fragrance-shopping behavior—not only whether the screens look refined.

Discovery language

Interview fragrance enthusiasts to learn whether they begin with notes, brands, occasions, moods, seasons, or community recommendations.

Comparison tasks

Test whether users can quickly compare longevity, projection, price, and dominant accords across multiple fragrances.

Collection model

Validate the categories users need—owned, sampled, wish list, decluttered, seasonal rotation, and signature scents.

Accessibility and commerce

Audit contrast, text scaling, keyboard flow, and product-state messaging before connecting the concept to purchasing or inventory data.

Reflection

What Aurea demonstrates.

Aurea gave me space to explore how visual direction and product clarity can reinforce one another.

The project strengthened my ability to create a distinctive interface language, sequence dense information, and design a cohesive experience across discovery, evaluation, storytelling, and retention. It also clarified an important limitation: visual polish should create a strong hypothesis, but user evidence must determine whether the final product works.
