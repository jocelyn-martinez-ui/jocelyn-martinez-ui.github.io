01 / THE BRIEF

An unrecorded check-in
isn’t an absence.

A failed check-in cannot tell an instructor why a student was not recorded. Treating that failure as an absence makes a technical problem into a consequential decision.

Who it is for

Students who need to understand or challenge a record, and faculty who need to review exceptions without losing the context of a class session.

What it should change

Make the state of a class session and its attendance record understandable to both roles; preserve a human correction path.

Evidence, contribution & constraints

The original case identifies a university capstone. This refinement uses a heuristic review of its roles and state model. No new student interviews, institutional policy review or campus pilot were conducted.

Four illustrative student records; one shared session; no authentication, location collection, institutional integration or durable audit storage.

01

One record, two responsibilities

Separate student and faculty interfaces use the same sample attendance state.

The alternative & the tradeoff

A single dashboard for both roles would reduce layout work but expose irrelevant controls to students.

Role switching is demonstrated locally; production authorization still requires a backend.

02

Review before consequence

Changing a faculty status opens a form showing the current and proposed values. The previous record stays intact until a reason is saved.

The alternative & the tradeoff

An inline status select is quicker, but it hides the rationale for a high-consequence change.

One additional step is justified by the need to explain the decision.

03

Verification has a boundary

The consent screen distinguishes a temporary verification input from the attendance result. This concept requests no location.

The alternative & the tradeoff

A frictionless permission prompt is shorter but asks for trust before explaining the purpose.

A real deployment needs non-location access, retention rules and institutional approval.

Beyond the ideal path

Interaction states and recovery
Open sessionStudent can enter the verification demonstration.
Closed sessionCheck-in is disabled; the correction path remains available.
Needs reviewWritten student context accompanies the record.
Correction draftNo record change until the reason is saved; cancellation leaves it intact.
Recorded changePrevious state, new state, actor and reason appear together.

Access is part of the interface

Use proper table headings and visible text for every status. Keep correction labels and error messages connected to their controls. Preserve a usable keyboard path through role switching and form submission. Never infer absence from a permission failure.

These are design provisions, not a conformance certification. Real-device, assistive-technology and user testing remain necessary.

04 / VISUAL DIRECTION

Institutional clarity,
without coldness.

A contemporary academic record: structured rosters for faculty, a focused class ticket for students, and an explicit decision trail connecting them.

Jakarta Sans + IBM Plex Mono

Plus Jakarta Sans for interface headings and body; IBM Plex Mono for record identifiers. Use 16–18 px body, 14 px metadata and tabular numbers. Titles stop at 44 px inside the application.

A consistent graphic language

Navy institutional rules, record numbers and an orange exception accent. Campaign typography centers student agency. Orange always accompanies a written status.

Component & motion rules

Dense but readable faculty tables; a narrow student workspace; a correction form that compares old and proposed states before committing. A record, session and review request remain separate concepts.

150 ms control feedback. A changed view moves focus to its heading. Confirmation is announced in a status region; no celebratory animation accompanies a consequential record change.

05 / REFLECTION

Test fairness,
not just completion.

The prototype demonstrates a defensible interaction model. It cannot establish attendance accuracy, student trust or institutional readiness.

The next validation questions

  • Ask students to explain the difference between a closed session and an attendance status.
  • Ask instructors to resolve an ambiguous review and explain why they chose that outcome.
  • Review data retention, access rights and non-location alternatives with campus stakeholders before any pilot.

No conversion, adoption, revenue or usability improvement is claimed for this concept.
