01 / THE BRIEF

A handoff needs
more than intent.

A kitchen may know what is ready, while a receiving partner needs quantities, timing and eligibility before committing. A warm message alone cannot resolve that coordination gap.

Who it is for

Kitchen operators preparing surplus food, community partners receiving it and an operations team coordinating a handoff. Funder communication is a supporting brand audience.

What it should change

Connect a clear operational record with an energetic, dignified public identity. Make the next responsibility visible without presenting a draft as a booked pickup.

Evidence, contribution & constraints

Self-directed service and identity exploration. Audience needs are hypotheses drawn from the project brief, not verified partner interviews. No actual charity, food-rescue operations or impact results are represented.

A local sample record with four form stages and a manually advanced journey. No food-safety certification, dispatch, partner acceptance, live capacity or driver tracking.

01

Eligibility comes first

The form pauses when the organization has not cleared the food for donation.

The alternative & the tradeoff

Beginning with quantity is quicker, but allows an inappropriate handoff to appear ready.

The product cannot certify food safety; responsibility stays with the organization.

02

A draft is not a booking

Preferred timing is displayed alongside a written statement that no pickup is arranged.

The alternative & the tradeoff

A celebratory confirmed label feels complete but creates false operational certainty.

The receipt acknowledges captured information, not a real dispatch.

03

Change is part of a service

Editing preserves the existing record until a revised version is saved. Cancellation restores the prior view.

The alternative & the tradeoff

A second new record is easy to create but risks duplicated work and ambiguous instructions.

Revision numbers expose change without pretending to implement production version control.

DESIGNED & IMPLEMENTED

  • Four-stage handoff form with preserved input and validation
  • Editable confirmed record with cancel and explicit revision
  • Partner-status language that distinguishes preference from booking
  • A typographic partner brief connecting print and digital instructions

Beyond the ideal path

Interaction states and recovery
Eligibility unknownPause the flow and direct the operator to their organization.
Invalid quantityRequire a whole number within the sample range.
Review pendingState that no pickup has been booked.
EditingKeep the last record intact until save; offer cancel.
RevisedUpdate one record and increment its visible revision.

Access is part of the interface

Maintain strong contrast on the forest and paper surfaces. Keep uppercase display type out of long instructions. Label each form input; connect errors to the relevant step. Never communicate arrival or eligibility through color alone.

These are design provisions, not a conformance certification. Real-device, assistive-technology and user testing remain necessary.

04 / VISUAL DIRECTION

Neighborhood energy.
Operational precision.

A neighborhood logistics language: bold condensed headlines, field labels, handoff dockets and directional sequencing. The identity should survive a low-cost printout as well as a campaign poster.

Barlow Condensed + Space Grotesk

Barlow Condensed for large uppercase messages; Space Grotesk for interface text; IBM Plex Mono for dispatch labels. Body 16–18 px, form labels 16 px and 48 px minimum action height.

A consistent graphic language

Forest green carries trust; coral identifies moments of action; yellow supports labeling. Strong horizontal rules and modular numbering replace decorative gradients. One-color layouts remain usable.

Component & motion rules

A practical partner desk with eligibility, description, timing and review. A confirmed sample remains editable; an edit is a draft until saved. The receipt distinguishes a preferred window from an arranged pickup.

Quick 120 ms control feedback. Progress changes only after a successful step. The simulated journey advances manually; no fake real-time pulsing location dot.

05 / REFLECTION

Validate the service
behind the screen.

The concept unites a memorable identity with a usable information handoff. Real eligibility rules, service ownership and partner capacity must be established before implementation.

The next validation questions

  • Observe kitchen operators completing the form during a realistic interruption.
  • Ask receiving partners what they need to accept or decline a handoff.
  • Test whether the receipt is clearly understood as pending coordination rather than a confirmed pickup.

No conversion, adoption, revenue or usability improvement is claimed for this concept.
