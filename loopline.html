THE BRIEF

THE CHALLENGE

Food rescue has a logistics problem—and a language problem.

Loopline needed to feel efficient enough for kitchen operators, trustworthy enough for funders, and human enough for neighbors. The identity could not simply decorate the service; it had to make the service easier to understand and use.

PROJECT MANDATE

  • 01Unify three audiences without flattening their needs.
  • 02Work from polished decks to low-cost office prints.
  • 03Turn operational complexity into clear next steps.
  • 04Leave behind a system the client can actually own.
02

THE SYSTEM AT WORK

ONE IDEA / MULTIPLE CONDITIONS

Flexible enough to travel.
Disciplined enough to stay recognizable.

The application system is built from repeatable moves: oversized action language, route-based lines, modular data blocks, and a consistent information footer.

Partner field kit

Built for counters, clipboards, and real working hands.

A modular print system makes the brand recognizable without slowing down production. High-contrast hierarchy, oversized action language, and a one-ink fallback keep every piece useful in imperfect environments.

  • CMYK + 1-color

MEASUREMENT PLAN

CONCEPT PROJECT / TARGETS, NOT CLAIMS

Define what success means before choosing what to polish.

Because Loopline is a concept, these are proposed launch targets—not fabricated outcomes. A real engagement would baseline each metric during discovery.

Target median time to schedule a first pickup.

80%completion

Target partner onboarding completion rate.

AAminimum

Accessibility target for core digital interactions.

2×faster

Target reduction in routine asset-production time.

THE DESIGN PRINCIPLE

“The difference between a good-looking brand and a useful one is whether the decisions still hold when the conditions change.”

Loopline was designed as infrastructure: expressive enough to earn attention, systematic enough to move across channels, and simple enough for a real team to maintain.
