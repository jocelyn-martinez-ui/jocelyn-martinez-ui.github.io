THE BRIEF

THE CHALLENGE

Food rescue has a logistics problem—and a language problem.

Loopline needed to feel efficient enough for kitchen operators, trustworthy enough for funders, and human enough for neighbors. The identity could not simply decorate the service; it had to make the service easier to understand and use.

PROJECT MANDATE

  • 01Unify three audiences without flattening their needs.
  • 02Work from polished decks to low-cost office prints.
  • 03Turn operational complexity into clear next steps.
  • 04Leave behind a system the client can actually own.

CONCEPT EXPLORATION + CRITIQUE

DECISION HISTORY / WHAT DID NOT SHIP

Three directions entered critique.
One survived the system test.

Because Loopline is a self-directed concept, this section does not invent client feedback. Instead, each route was reviewed against a consistent studio critique: clarity, distinctiveness, small-scale performance, motion potential, and low-cost reproduction.

REJECTED / A
CLOSED LOOP
FAMILIAR / TOO GENERIC

A circular-arrow metaphor.

Immediate sustainability recognition, but it read as recycling software and erased the human handoff.

  • Strong at small sizes
  • Low category distinction
  • No clear source or destination
REJECTED / B
SHARED TABLE
HUMAN / TOO STATIC

Three seats around one table.

Warm and community-led, but the symbol felt stationary, lost legibility below 28 px, and resisted motion.

  • Strong emotional cue
  • Weak logistics story
  • Fragile one-color detail
REJECTED / C
ROUTE MONOGRAM
DISTINCT / OVER-ENGINEERED

An interlocking “LL” route.

Ownable in isolation, but the reading required explanation and the tight turns filled in during low-cost output.

  • High brand ownership
  • Slow first read
  • Poor office-print survival

SELECTED / OPEN HANDOFF

The final mark does more with less.

One open turn establishes forward motion. Three nodes carry the operating story. A single-weight route survives 16 px, one ink, animation, signage, and a sponsor deck without changing meaning.

Loopline was designed as infrastructure: expressive enough to earn attention, systematic enough to move across channels, and simple enough for a real team to maintain.
