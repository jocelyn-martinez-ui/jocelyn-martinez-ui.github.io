01 / THE BRIEF

A voice
before a rating.

Choosing what to read can become a performance of ratings and endless browsing. A quieter experience should help someone encounter a voice, keep a book and return without pressure.

Who it is for

Readers who choose by mood or writing style and want a personal collection without a social feed, public rating or streak mechanic.

What it should change

Help readers move from curiosity to an excerpt, a shelf and a saved passage while preserving control of the experience.

Evidence, contribution & constraints

Self-directed concept with a directional review of reading products in the original brief. No formal reader interviews or discovery-preference study were conducted. Book titles and excerpt text are original concept material.

Three original book studies and short excerpts. No licensed full books, recommendation engine, reading-time measurement, social profiles or cloud synchronization.

01

Blind discovery stays optional

A reader can hide cover and rating cues for one excerpt, then reveal the title deliberately.

The alternative & the tradeoff

Making blind discovery mandatory creates a stronger concept but obstructs readers who want to scan titles.

The alternative route offers a different reading experience without withholding the main catalogue.

02

A shelf belongs near the decision

Book details offer Want to read, Reading and Finished directly. Choosing one saves the book and its state.

The alternative & the tradeoff

A separate organization screen is tidier but makes a simple intent harder to express.

Three explicit states add controls while reducing ambiguity about a generic Save action.

03

A quotation retains its source

Saved passages carry their book title and a return path; removal can be undone.

The alternative & the tradeoff

Detached quote cards travel easily but strip away context and make the source harder to recover.

The collection prioritizes personal reading context over social sharing.

Beyond the ideal path

Interaction states and recovery
No matching titlesOffer a clear filter reset.
Blind excerptKeep the title hidden until the reader chooses reveal.
Shelf chosenSave the book and show the current reading state.
Reader limitsDisable type controls at the defined size bounds.
Passage removedOffer undo and restore the original source association.

Access is part of the interface

Use real text rather than images for prose, generous line spacing and explicit reader controls. Maintain text contrast in both themes. Keyboard users can reveal a book, save a passage and move a title between shelves.

These are design provisions, not a conformance certification. Real-device, assistive-technology and user testing remain necessary.

Newsreader + Libre Baskerville

Newsreader for editorial hierarchy; Libre Baskerville for prose. Reader size adjusts from 18–30 px. Keep line length near 55–65 characters and line-height around 1.7–1.85.

A consistent graphic language

Book-cover studies, editorial folios, rules and passage quotations with attribution. The visual motif is the page, not a row of indistinguishable app cards.

Component & motion rules

Mood and genre filters remain optional. Blind reading is a secondary path with a reveal action. Book details can directly save a title to Want to read, Reading or Finished; passages keep their source and support undo.

Gentle 180 ms color changes with no autoplay page turning. Reader controls respond immediately. Changing a shelf announces the result without a streak, score or celebration.

05 / REFLECTION

Understand how
readers return.

The concept demonstrates an editorial interface and low-pressure discovery. It does not establish reading retention, recommender quality or long-session comfort.

The next validation questions

  • Compare direct catalogue browsing and blind discovery for different reader intentions.
  • Test whether shelf labels are understood without implying measured reading completion.
  • Evaluate sustained readability, text resizing and dark mode with readers using different devices and visual settings.

No conversion, adoption, revenue or usability improvement is claimed for this concept.
