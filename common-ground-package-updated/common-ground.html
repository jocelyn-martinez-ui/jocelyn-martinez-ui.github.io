01 / Overview

The assignment

Design a report that can carry statistics without losing warmth.

Common Ground explores the relationship between cultural participation, public space, and communication. The design challenge was to combine long-form reading, atmospheric imagery, charts, tables, profiles, a timeline, recommendations, endnotes, and production information inside one coherent publication.

The report was designed as a complete system rather than a sequence of unrelated spreads. Repeated margins, baseline rhythm, folios, running heads, caption rules, color roles, and image treatments create continuity while allowing individual sections to change pace.

03 / Grid & master pages

The structure beneath the spreads

A flexible 12-column grid anchored to a 14-point baseline.

The grid supports quiet single-column reading, dense multi-column analysis, narrow caption zones, full-width imagery, and asymmetric opening pages. It creates consistency without forcing every spread into the same composition.

05 / Data & information design

Statistics that belong to the publication

Charts, tables, and timelines share the same editorial language.

The data system avoids decorative dashboards. Values remain visible, labels stay close to their marks, and each visual includes enough explanatory context to work in print or when separated into a presentation or social asset.

Write values explicitlyReaders should not need to estimate a percentage from the length or angle of a mark.
Keep scales consistentComparable charts use stable lengths, spacing, and label placement.
Separate finding from source noteThe narrative takeaway is prominent; methodology and limitations remain available.
Design for reuseThe same visual can move into an executive summary, presentation, or social cutdown.

08 / Reflection

What the project demonstrates

Editorial consistency comes from relationships, not repetition.

Common Ground strengthened my ability to balance detail and storytelling across a sustained document. The strongest spreads are visually different, but they still feel related because their margins, type roles, color behavior, captions, running heads, and folios follow one underlying system.

01

Strongest contribution

A complete 28-page publication that moves between narrative, data, photography, tables, profiles, and production content without losing cohesion.

02

Largest design risk

Quiet editorial styling can become too sparse or reduce practical reading cues when hierarchy and contrast are not tested carefully.

03

Next iteration

Rebuild the report natively in InDesign, run a full preflight, print a bound proof, refine color on paper, and test the screen PDF for accessibility.
